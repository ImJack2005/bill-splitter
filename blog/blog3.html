Venmo vs Splitwise: Which Should Your Friend Group Use?

📅 June 2026 · ⏱ 6 min read

Your friend group just got back from a weekend trip. Someone paid for the Airbnb, someone covered the grocery run, someone bought gas twice, and the dinner receipts are piling up in a group chat nobody wants to do math in. Two apps come up every time: Venmo and Splitwise. Everyone has one opinion. Nobody fully understands the difference.

Here's the truth: Venmo and Splitwise are not really competing for the same job. They solve different problems, and the best groups use both — at the right moment. Understanding which does what will save you a lot of awkward "wait, did you send me that yet?" texts.

What Each App Actually Does

💙 Venmo
  • Sends and receives real money
  • Linked to your bank account or debit card
  • Has a social feed (optional)
  • Instant transfers between Venmo users
  • Owned by PayPal
💚 Splitwise
  • Tracks who owes what
  • Does not move money itself
  • Organizes shared expenses over time
  • Handles complex multi-person debts
  • Works across currencies

In plain terms: Splitwise is a ledger. Venmo is a wallet. Splitwise keeps score. Venmo settles it.

Most people try to use Venmo for both — and that's where it breaks down. Venmo is excellent at sending $23 to your roommate. It is terrible at tracking who owes what across a four-day camping trip with eight people and a shared grocery budget.

When Venmo Wins

Venmo is the right tool when the transaction is simple, immediate, and involves two people who both have the app and US bank accounts.

  • Your friend covered your movie ticket — send them $15
  • You split a Lyft — Venmo your share right now
  • Dinner for two where you grabbed the check — your friend sends you half
  • Paying back someone quickly before you forget
Venmo scenario

You and a coworker grab lunch. She pays the $34 check. You open Venmo, search her name, type $17, add a taco emoji, send. Done in 45 seconds.

Venmo is perfect here. There's nothing to track. One transaction, two people, settled immediately.

Venmo's weakness shows up the moment anything gets complicated — multiple people, multiple expenses, or anything that spans more than one day. Its transaction history is a flat list, not a balance sheet. You can't easily see "overall, who owes me money across all these transactions."

When Splitwise Wins

Splitwise is the right tool when expenses pile up across time, people, and categories — and you want to know the net amount owed at the end, not chase individual payments.

  • A weekend trip where different people cover different things
  • Shared apartment expenses between roommates
  • A friend group that eats out regularly and rotates who pays
  • International travel with a mixed-currency group
  • Any situation where you need a running tally, not just a single transaction
Splitwise scenario

Six friends go to Nashville for a long weekend. Marcus books the Airbnb ($720). Priya buys groceries twice ($95, $63). You pay for the Friday night bar tab ($180). Jordan covers the country music show tickets ($210).

Without Splitwise, settling this requires a group chat argument, someone's questionable spreadsheet, and at least one person feeling shortchanged.

With Splitwise: everyone logs their expenses as they go. At the end, the app calculates the minimum number of payments needed to settle everything. Instead of six people sending money to five other people, you might end up with just three transactions total.

That last point — minimizing the number of transactions needed to settle a group — is Splitwise's superpower. It's called debt simplification, and it's genuinely impressive once you've seen it work on a messy weekend trip.

The Privacy Thing Nobody Talks About

Venmo has a social feed. By default, your transactions are visible to your friends — the amount is hidden, but the emoji and note are public. That note that says "🍕🍺 Friday" is visible to everyone who follows you both.

Most people don't realize this until a nosy family member or employer sees their feed. You can set all transactions to private in Venmo's settings, and you absolutely should if this bothers you. But it's opt-out, not opt-in.

Splitwise has no social feed. Your debts are between you and the people in your group, and nobody else can see them. For a lot of people — especially for larger amounts or sensitive expenses — this matters.

The Fees Question

Both apps are free for core use, but the fine print differs:

Venmo: Free to send money from a bank account or debit card. Sending from a credit card costs 3%. Instant transfer to your bank costs 1.75% (minimum $0.25, max $25). Standard bank transfer is free but takes 1–3 days.

Splitwise: Free for unlimited expense tracking. The paid tier (Splitwise Pro, around $3.99/month) adds receipt scanning, currency conversion, and charts. For most casual users, the free version is plenty.

Can You Use Both at the Same Time?

Yes — and this is actually the best approach for most friend groups.

Use Splitwise to track everything throughout the trip or month. Log every expense as it happens. At the end, Splitwise tells you exactly who owes what to whom, in the most simplified form possible.

Then use Venmo to settle those final balances. Splitwise even has a built-in Venmo integration — you can tap "Settle up" and it opens Venmo with the amount pre-filled.

The workflow that actually works

1. Add the group to Splitwise before the trip starts.

2. Log every shared expense in Splitwise as it happens — whoever paid, what it was, who was involved.

3. At the end, check Splitwise for the final "you owe / you're owed" summary.

4. Settle those specific amounts via Venmo in one round of payments.

What About Cash App, Zelle, and PayPal?

Briefly, since they come up:

  • Zelle — built into most US bank apps, sends money directly between bank accounts with no app needed. Fast and fee-free, but no group tracking whatsoever. Use it like Venmo for simple payments.
  • Cash App — similar to Venmo, popular in younger demographics and among users who don't want a PayPal connection. No group expense tracking.
  • PayPal — the original, now mostly used for business payments and online shopping. Feels clunky for splitting dinner.

None of these replace Splitwise for group tracking. They're all payment tools, not expense management tools.

The Honest Answer

If your friend group is choosing between Venmo and Splitwise, you're asking the wrong question. They're not substitutes — they're complements.

For a single dinner, Venmo is faster and simpler. For anything involving multiple people, multiple expenses, or multiple days, Splitwise does the heavy lifting that Venmo was never designed to do. And when it's time to actually move money, Venmo handles the final step in seconds.

The groups that never argue about money are usually the ones who figured out this two-app workflow early. Track in Splitwise, pay in Venmo. That's the system.
